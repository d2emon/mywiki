# Klingon grammar

The grammar of the [Klingon language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_language "Klingon language") was created by [Marc Okrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Okrand "Marc Okrand") for the _[Star Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek "Star Trek")_ franchise. He first described it in his book _[The Klingon Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Klingon_Dictionary "The Klingon Dictionary")_. It is a [nominative–accusative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominative%E2%80%93accusative "Nominative–accusative"), primarily suffixing [agglutinative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agglutinative "Agglutinative") language, and has an [object–verb–subject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object%E2%80%93verb%E2%80%93subject "Object–verb–subject") word order. The Klingon language has a number of unusual grammatical features, as it was designed to sound and seem alien, but it has an extremely regular morphology.

## Word order

Klingon follows an [object–verb–subject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object%E2%80%93verb%E2%80%93subject "Object–verb–subject") word order.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-1) Adverbs usually go at the beginning of the sentence[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p56-2) and prepositional phrases go before the object.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-3)

`Doʼ DujDaq ghoqwIʼ Sam laʼ`

| Doʼ         | Duj  | -   | Daq | ghoqwIʼ | Sam  | laʼ       |
| ----------- | ---- | --- | --- | ------- | ---- | --------- |
| fortunately | ship | +   | LOC | spy     | find | commander |

Fortunately, the commander found the spy aboard the ship

Sentences can be treated as objects, and the word `ʼeʼ` is placed after the sentence. `ʼeʼ` is treated as the object of the next sentence.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-4) The adverbs, indirect objects and locatives of the latter sentence go after the subject, but before the `ʼeʼ`[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-5)

`bIpIv ʼeʼ vItuʼ`

| bI     | -   | pIv        | ʼeʼ  | vI   | -   | tuʼ     |
| ------ | --- | ---------- | ---- | ---- | --- | ------- |
| you-Ø‍ | +   | be healthy | that | I-it | +   | observe |

I see that you're healthy (lit. I observe that you are healthy)

## Nouns

Klingon has three [noun classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noun_class "Noun class"). The first one is living beings with an innate capacity to use language. The second one is body parts (not the body itself) and the third is all other nouns.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-Okrand_1992,_p.22-6) Klingon has no articles, so the word `**raS**` _table_ can mean _a_ table or _the_ table. The difference between the two is inferred from context.

### Suffixes

There are five types of noun suffixes. A word cannot have two suffixes of the same type.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p21-7) The suffixes are ordered based on type number; a type 2 suffix goes before a type 3 suffix, but after a type 1 suffix.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-8) In the following example, all five suffix types are used in the correct order.

`vengHommeyqoqchajDaq`

| veng | -   | Hom | -   | mey | -   | qoq | -   | chaj  | -   | Daq |
| ---- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ----- | --- | --- |
| city | +   | DIM | +   | PL  | +   | DUB | +   | their | +   | LOC |

in their so-called villages

#### Type 1 (size, affection)

This type has three suffixes:

- The [augmentative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentative "Augmentative") suffix`-ʼaʼ`, 
  ghom → ghom**ʼaʼ**
  group → crowd
- The [diminutive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminutive "Diminutive") suffix `-Hom`,[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p21-7)
  yuQ → yuQ**Hom**
  planet → planetoid
- and the endearment suffix **`-oy`**.
  vav → vav**oy**
  father → daddy
    - If the noun to which the endearment suffix is added ends with a vowel, a glottal stop is inserted between them:[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-9)
      ghu → ghu**ʼoy**
      baby → dear baby

#### Type 2 (plurals)

This type of suffix forms plurals. There are three suffixes, one for each noun class.

- The suffix `-puʼ` is for beings capable of using language.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-Okrand_1992,_p.22-6)
  qetwIʼ → qetwIʼ**puʼ**
  runner→ runners
- The suffix `-Duʼ` is for body parts,
  ghop→ ghop**Duʼ**
  hand→ hands
- The suffix `-mey` is used for all other nouns.
  quS → quS**mey**
  chair → chairs
- When `-mey` is used for nouns that would normally take `-puʼ` or `-Duʼ`, it carries the connotation of being all over the place.
  ghot → ghot**mey**
  person → people all over the place

A noun does not require a plural suffix if a pronoun, pronominal prefix, or context serves to indicate that it is plural,[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-10) or if it is being used in conjunction with a number.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p55-11)

`Duypuʼ chaH` or `Duy chaH`

_They are emissaries._

`raSmey DIghor` or `raS DIghor`

_We broke the tables._

#### Type 3 (accuracy)

This type of suffix indicates the speaker's opinion of the applicability of the noun. There are three suffixes:

- The suffix `-qoq` indicates that the speaker thinks what they are referencing is not actually represented by the noun.
  QaH → QaH**qoq**
  the help → the so-called help
- The [dubitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubitative_mood "Dubitative mood") suffix `-Hey` indicates the speaker is not entirely sure if the object they are referencing is represented by the noun.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-12)
  choH → choH**Hey**
  change → apparent change
- The suffix `-naʼ` indicates that the speaker is entirely sure that the object is represented accurately by the noun.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p25-13)
  jup → jup**naʼ**
  friend → true friend

#### Type 4 (possession, determiners)

This type of suffix indicates possession or specifies which object is referred to. It contains twelve suffixes.

There are ten possession suffixes, indicating who is the possessor of the object, which may be a person. For first- and second-person possessors, there are different forms depending on whether the "object" is a being capable of using language.

| Possessives[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p25-13) | 1st-person  <br>singular | 2nd-person  <br>singular | 3rd-person  <br>singular | 1st-person  <br>plural | 2nd-person  <br>plural | 3rd-person  <br>plural |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------ | ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Not capable of using language                                                     | `-wIj`                   | `-lIj`                   | `-Daj`                   | `-maj`                 | `-raj`                 | `-chaj`                |
| Capable of using language                                                         | `-wIʼ`                   | `-lIʼ`                   | `-Daj`                   | `-maʼ`                 | `-raʼ`                 | `-chaj`                |

There are also two determiner suffixes:[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-14)

- `-vam` _this_ indicates an object that is nearby or that is being discussed
- `-vetlh` _that_ indicates an object that is not nearby or that had previously been discussed

Examples:

- Non-language-user possessives:
  nav → nav**wIj**
  paper → my paper
- Language-user possessives:
  qeSwIʼpuʼ → qeSwIʼpuʼ**lIʼ**
  advisors → your advisors
- Determiners:
  Soj → Soj**vam**
  food → this food

#### Type 5 (syntactic role)

This type of suffix serves a syntactic role in the sentence. It contains five suffixes.

- The [locative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locative "Locative") suffix `-Daq` indicates the action of the sentence is taking place in, at or on the noun.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-Okrand_1992,_p.27-15) With certain verbs, it indicates motion towards the noun.
  juHmaj → juHmaj**Daq**
  our home → in our home
- The [ablative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ablative_case "Ablative case") suffix `-voʼ` indicates that the action is taking place away from the noun. Again, with certain verbs, it indicates motion away from the noun.
  qoj → qoj**voʼ**
  cliff → away from the cliff
- The [causal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal_case "Causal case") suffix `-moʼ` indicates that the action is occurring because of the noun.
  ghuʼ → ghuʼ**moʼ**
  situation → because of the situation
- The [dative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dative_case "Dative case")/[benefactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benefactive_case "Benefactive case") suffix `-vaD` indicates the indirect object, and/or the noun for which the action has been done.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-Okrand_1992,_p.27-15)
  jeʼwIʼ → jeʼwIʼ**vaD**
  buyer → to/for the buyer
- The [topicalizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topic%E2%80%93comment "Topic–comment") suffix `-ʼeʼ` indicates the topic of the sentence or emphasises the noun in the phrase, and also marks the head noun of a relative clause.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-16)

## Verbs

Klingon verbs mark for [aspect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_aspect "Grammatical aspect") but not for tense, which is indicated where necessary by context and by time adverbs. Prefixes mark subject and object. There are ten types of suffix, and as with nouns, a verb can have no more than one suffix of any type. (The tenth type, called _rovers_, are an exception.) Again as with nouns, the types of suffix must appear in a strict order, indicated by their type number: a type 2 suffix goes before a type 3 suffix, but after a type 1 suffix. A rover suffix can go between any of them.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p44-17)

Unlike English, there is no infinitive.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-Okrand_1992,_p.33-18) The presentation of the verb stem as an infinitive in this article's example sentences is just to show the individual morphemes.

### Suffixes

#### Type 1 (reflexive/reciprocal)

This type of suffix forms reflexive verbs. There are two suffixes.

- The [reflexive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexive_verb "Reflexive verb") suffix `-ʼegh` indicates that the individual subject(s) does/do the action to her/him/itself/themselves.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-21)
  jIlegh → jIlegh**ʼegh**
  I see → I see myself
  
  maʼang → maʼang**ʼegh**
  We reveal → We reveal ourselves (individually)
- The [reciprocal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_\(grammar\) "Reciprocal (grammar)") suffix `-chuq` indicates that the individual subjects do the action to each other.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p36-22) Intransitive verbs cannot take this suffix.
  Sulegh → Sulegh**chuq**
  You(pl) see → You(pl) see each other

#### Type 2 (volition/necessity)

This type of suffix deals with the subject's volition. There are five suffixes.

- The suffix `-nIS` indicates that the subject is required to or has the necessity to complete the action.
  chojaʼ → chojaʼ**nIS**
  You tell me → You need to tell me
- The suffix `-qang` indicates that the subject is willing to perform the action.
  lungev → lungev**qang**
  They sell it → They are willing to sell it
- The suffix `-rup` indicates the subject is prepared to complete the action.
  ghuʼ poj → ghuʼ poj**rup**
  He/She analyzes the situation → He/She is ready to analyze the situation
- The suffix `-beH` indicates that the subject has been set up to complete the action. `-beH` is used with devices, whereas `-rup` is used with beings.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p36-22)
  ghoghlIj qon → ghoghlIj qon**beH**
  It records your voice → It is ready to record your voice
- The suffix `-vIp` indicates that the subject is afraid to do the action.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p37-23)
  bImej → bImej**vIp**
  You leave → You are afraid to leave

#### Type 3 ([inceptive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inceptive "Inceptive")/[inchoative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inchoative "Inchoative"))

This type of suffix describes the action of the verb. There are two suffixes.

- The suffix `-choH` indicates a change of state to that indicated by the verb:
  yIt → yIt**choH**
  He/she/it walks → He/she/it starts walking
  
  Doq → Doq**choH**
  It is red → It becomes red
- The suffix `-qaʼ` indicates the action had been stopped, but is now resuming, or that the action is being performed again.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p37-23)
  yIQong → yIQong**qaʼ**
  Sleep! → Go back to sleep!

#### Type 4 (causative)

There is only one suffix in this category, the [causative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causative "Causative") suffix `-moH`. This suffix indicates that the subject is causing something to happen. If the verb to which it is added is transitive, the object becomes the indirect object. Many Klingon words are derived this way. For example, the verb _clean_ (`SayʼmoH`) is derived from the verb _be clean_ (`Sayʼ`).[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p38-24)

**Intransitive verb**:

`poS lojmIt.`

| poS     | lojmIt |
| ------- | ------ |
| be open | door   |

The door is open.

Causative form:

`lojmIt poSmoH qup.`

| lojmIt | poS     | -   | moH  | qup<br> |
| ------ | ------- | --- | ---- | ------- |
| door   | be open | +   | CAUS | elder   |

The elder opened the door. (lit. The elder caused the door to be open.)

**Transitive verb**:

`paq Danej.`

| paq  | Da     | -   | nej      |
| ---- | ------ | --- | -------- |
| book | you-it | +   | look for |

You look/are looking for the book. (lit. You look for the book.)

Causative form:

`paq qanejmoH`

| paq  | qa    | -   | nej      | -   | moH  |
| ---- | ----- | --- | -------- | --- | ---- |
| book | I-you | +   | look for | +   | CAUS |

I made you look for the book. (lit. I caused you to look for the book.)

#### Type 5 (undefined subject; capability)

There are two unrelated suffixes in this group. The suffix `-luʼ` indicates an undefined subject.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p38-24) The verb prefixes that are normally used for first or second person _subject_ with third person singular _object_ are used to indicate first or second person _object_. The suffix `-laH` indicates that the subject is capable of performing the action of the verb.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p38-24)

Examples:

- `-luʼ` without prefix:

`much bejluʼ`

| much         | bej   | -   | luʼ      |
| ------------ | ----- | --- | -------- |
| presentation | watch | +   | INDF SBJ |

the presentation is watched, someone watches the presentation [[Note 1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-25)

`much bej`

_He/she watches the presentation_

- `-luʼ` with prefix:

`vItlhaʼluʼ`

| vI   | -   | tlhaʼ | -   | luʼ      |
| ---- | --- | ----- | --- | -------- |
| I-it | +   | chase | +   | INDF SBJ |

I am chased, someone chases me

`vItlhaʼ`

_I chase it_

- `-laH`:

`vIbomlaH`

| vI   | -   | bom  | -   | laH     |
| ---- | --- | ---- | --- | ------- |
| I-it | +   | sing | +   | able to |

I can sing it

#### Type 6 (perfection; uncertainty)

This type indicates the speaker's opinion of the action of the verb. There are four suffixes.

- The suffix `-chuʼ` indicates that the speaker considers the action is done in the best possible manner.
  pIQoy → pIQoy**chuʼ**
  we hear you → we hear you clearly
- The suffix `-bej` indicates that the speaker is completely sure the action is occurring.
  SuQeH → SuQeH**bej**
  you (plural) are angry → you are definitely angry
- The [dubitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubitative_mood "Dubitative mood") suffix `-lawʼ` indicates that the speaker thinks the action is occurring, but is not sure.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-26)
  luyaj → luyaj**lawʼ**
  they understand it → they seem to understand it
- The suffix `-baʼ` indicates that the speaker thinks that it is obvious that the action is occurring.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p175-27)
  lupar → lupar**baʼ**
  they dislike it → they obviously dislike it

#### Type 7 (aspect)

This type indicates the verb's [aspect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_aspect "Grammatical aspect"). There are four Type 7 suffixes.

Note that aspect is different from tense and independent of it. A "completed" event ([perfective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfective "Perfective") aspect, `-puʼ` or `-taʼ`) can just as easily be set before, during, or after the time of description (past, present, or future tense), or unspecified for tense. For simplicity, this section says "is completed", not "was, is, or will be completed." (Do not confuse perfective aspect with ["perfectly done"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#Type_6_\(perfection;_uncertainty\)).)

- The [perfective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfective "Perfective") suffix `-puʼ` indicates that, in the time context of the sentence, the action is completed.
  qaS → qaS**puʼ**
  It occurs → It has occurred
  
  vIghor → vIghor**puʼ**
  I break it → I have broken it

- The suffix `-taʼ` also indicates that the action is completed, and further specifies that it was done on purpose.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-28)
  Qu' QIj → Qu' QIj**taʼ**
  She explains the mission → She has explained the mission
  
  vIghor → vIghor**taʼ**
  I break it → I have broken it (on purpose)
- The suffix `-taH` indicates that the verb is occurring continuously.
  jItlhuH → jItlhuH**taH**
  I breathe → I keep breathing
- The suffix `-lIʼ` indicates that the verb is occurring continuously, but that it has a definite ending point.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p42-29)
  megh vIvut → megh vIvut**lIʼ**
  I prepare lunch → I am preparing lunch

The perfective aspect can also be indicated by the use of the verb form `rIntaH` after the main verb. This carries the connotation of irreversibility.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p42-29)

`ghorluʼ rIntaH` _It has been broken (and it cannot be mended)_

#### Type 8 (honorific)

There is only one suffix in this group, the [honorific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorific "Honorific") suffix `-neS`. It is used when addressing any type of superior, be it social, political, or military, and only when being very polite or having high regard for that person. It is never required.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p43-30)

- `-neS`:
  qaqIH → qaqIH**neS**
  I meet you → I am honoured to meet you

#### Type 9 (syntactic)

Eleven suffixes specify syntactic roles in the sentence.

##### [Nominalizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominalization#Derivational_morphology_and_nominalization "Nominalization")

Two suffixes form specific types of noun from a verb.

- The suffix `-wIʼ` is used to form words for [persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_noun "Agent noun") and things that do something, much as English nouns of the form _X-er_ can mean either "person who does X" (_listener, baker_) or "thing that does X" (_screwdriver, sprinkler_).[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p44-17)
  ʼIj → ʼIj**wIʼ**
  listen → listener
  
  woch → woch**wIʼ**
  be tall → tall person or tall thing
- The suffix `-ghach` is used as a [nominalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominalization "Nominalization") for verbs ending in suffixes, which otherwise are unable to be nominalized. This suffix is usually used with other suffixes and is rarely found alone with the verb stem.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-HQv3n3p10t13-31)[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-32)
  QallaH → QallaH**ghach**
  be able to swim → the ability to swim
  
  pIvchoH → pIvchoH**ghach**
  become healthy → becoming healthy

##### Modals

These two suffixes inflect the verb in specific [grammatical moods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_mood "Grammatical mood").

- The [interrogative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interrogative "Interrogative") suffix `-ʼaʼ` is used to form yes–no questions.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p44-17)
  DIboQnIS → DIboQnIS**ʼaʼ**
  We need to assist them → Do we need to assist them?
- The [optative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optative "Optative") suffix `-jaj` is used to indicate a wish or desire of the speaker.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p175-27)
  bIQap → bIQap**jaj**
  You succeed → May you succeed

##### Subordinators

The following seven suffixes are used to form [subordinate clauses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_clause "Dependent clause"). A subordinate clause may go after or before the clause it modifies.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-33)

_**Time**_

- The suffix `-paʼ` indicates that the event described in the main clause occurs chronologically before the event of this clause.
  `jItlheDpaʼ, HIboQ` _Before I depart, assist me_
  (`jItlheD` _I depart_, `HIboQ` _assist me_)
- The suffix `-vIS` indicates that the main clause is occurring at the same time as this clause. It is always used in conjunction with the type-7 suffix continuous suffix `-taH`.
  `lumtaHvIS, pagh taʼ` _He accomplishes nothing while he procrastinates_
  (`lum` _procrastinate_, `taʼ` _accomplish_, `pagh` _nothing_)
- The suffix `-DIʼ` indicates that the event of the main clause occurs immediately after the event of this clause is completed.
  `jImej chocholDIʼ` _As soon as you approach me, I leave_
  (`jImej` _I leave_, `chochol` _you approach me_)

_**Cause and effect**_

- The suffix `-chugh` is used to form [conditionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_sentence "Conditional sentence").[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p43-30)
  `DaSamlaHchugh, DaSuqlaH` _If you can find it, you can take it_
  (`DaSamlaH` _you can find it_, `DaSuqlaH` _you can acquire (take) it_)
- The suffix `-moʼ` indicates the main clause is occurring because of this clause.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p175-27)
  `bIʼIlmoʼ, qavoq` _Because you are sincere, I trust you_
  (`bIʼIl` _you are sincere_, `qavoq` _I trust you_)

_**(Relative and purpose clauses)**_

- The suffix `-bogh` is used in relative clauses. Their usage is covered in the [relative clauses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#Relative_clauses) section.
- The suffix `-meH` is used in purpose clauses.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p44-17) Their usage is covered in the [purpose clauses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#Purpose_clauses) section.

#### Rovers

This type of suffix is known as a `lengwIʼ` in Klingon, which is translated as _rover_ (`leng` _wander_ + `wIʼ`). There are four rovers. These suffixes have no defined position, and can go after the verb stem or after any suffix – even another rover – except after a type-9 suffix or where the result would be meaningless. They modify whatever directly precedes them.

- The rover suffix `-beʼ` negates what precedes it but in the imperative mood [**-Qoʼ**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#-Qo7) is used.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-34)
  wInaD → wInaD**beʼ**
  We praise it → We do not praise it
  
  boʼollaH → boʼollaH**beʼ**
  You are able to verify it → You are not able to verify it
- The rover suffix `-Qoʼ` negates what precedes it in the imperative mood. In the indicative mood it indicates refusal by the subject. It can only be used between verb suffixes of Type 8 and Type 9.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p47-35)
  yIQIp → yIQIp**Qoʼ**
  Be stupid → don't be stupid
  
  vIlon → vIlon**Qoʼ**
  I abandon it → I refuse to abandon it
- The rover suffix `-quʼ` emphasises what precedes it.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-36)
  narghqang → narghqang**quʼ**
  He is willing to escape → He is really willing to escape
- The rover suffix `-Haʼ` reverses what precedes it; that is, it indicates that the opposite of what precedes it is being done, or that the action is being undone.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p47-35) If used on a verb that cannot be undone and has no meaningful opposite, it means to perform the action wrongly, not in the proper way, somewhat like the English prefix _mis-_ in _misspeak, mistype, misspell_, etc. Unlike the other rovers, it can be placed only just after the stem; its classification in the rover category is attributed to the insistence of fictional Klingon grammarians.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p49-37)
  yIchuʼ → yIchuʼ**Haʼ**
  Activate it → De-activate it
  
  boloʼ → boloʼ**Haʼ**
  you (plural) use it → you (pl.) misuse it
  
  bIQuch → bIQuch**Haʼ**
  You are happy → You are unhappy

The position of the rover suffixes affects the meaning of the word. Contrast

`luSoplaH` _They are able to eat it_

`luSoplaHbeʼ` _They are not able to eat it_

`luSopbeʼlaH` _They are able to not eat it_

(In context, possibly equivalent to _They can refuse to eat it_)

`luSopbeʼlaHbeʼ` _They are not able to not eat it_

(In context, possibly equivalent to _They cannot refuse to eat it_)

### Pronouns and copula

Klingon has no verb that corresponds to the verb _to be_; the concept is expressed using a different grammatical construction. Pronouns can be used as verbs that act as the pronoun plus the verb to be. The pronoun can take verb suffixes, which then modify the pronoun like any other verb. A third-person subject that is not a pronoun must go after the pronoun-verb and carry the type-5 noun suffix `-ʼeʼ`[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p68-38)

| Pronouns[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-39) | 1st-person  <br>singular | 2nd-person  <br>singular | 3rd-person  <br>singular | 1st-person  <br>plural | 2nd-person  <br>plural | 3rd-person  <br>plural |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------ | ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Capable of using language                                                  | `jIH`                    | `SoH`                    | `ghaH`                   | `maH`                  | `tlhIH`                | `chaH`                 |
| Not capable of using language                                              |                          |                          | `ʼoH`                    |                        |                        | `bIH`                  |

Examples:

- `qonwI' tlhIH` _You are composers_
- `ghojwIʼ ghaH HaDwIʼʼeʼ` _A studier is a learner_

## Adjectives

Klingon does not have adjectives as a distinct part of speech. Instead, many intransitive verbs can be used as adjectives, in which case they follow the noun they modify. Contrast (`wep` _coat_, and `yIQ` _be wet_)

`wep yIQ`

_the wet coat_

with

`yIQ wep.`

_The coat is wet._

In this construction, the only verbal suffixes allowed are rover suffixes such as `-quʼ` and `-Haʼ`.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-40) Type-5 _noun_ suffixes that would normally be attached to the noun are instead attached to the adjectival verb:[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-41)

`paʼDaq`

_in the room_

`paʼ tInDaq`

_in the big room_ (`tIn` _big_)

## Adverbs

[Adverbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverb "Adverb") are usually placed at the beginning of the sentence,[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p56-2) but time adverbs go before other adverbs.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-42)

`wa'leS ghaytan nujatlh.`

| wa'leS   | ghaytan | nu      | -   | jatlh |
| -------- | ------- | ------- | --- | ----- |
| tomorrow | likely  | they-us | +   | speak |

They will probably speak to us tomorrow.

Adverbs can take the rover suffix `-Haʼ` to denote the opposite adverbial.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-43)

`Doʼ`

_fortunately_

`DoʼHaʼ`

_unfortunately_

## Conjunctions

Klingon has seven conjunctions, and they are different for nouns and for sentences. The noun conjunctions are `je` for a [logical conjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_conjunction "Logical conjunction") (_and_), `joq` for a [logical disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_disjunction "Logical disjunction") (_and/or_) and `ghap` for an [exclusive disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_disjunction "Exclusive disjunction") (_either/or_). Noun conjunctions go after the nouns they connect. Sentence conjunctions are the reverses of the noun conjunctions; `ʼej` for _and_, `qoj` for _and/or_, and `pagh` for _either/or_. `ʼach` (or `ʼa`) (_but_) is used to contrast sentences.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p55-11)

## Clauses

### Relative clauses

In a relative clause, the verb has the type-9 verb suffix `-bogh` added to it.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-Okrand_1992,_p.64-44) The order of the words in relative clauses remains the same as in regular clauses, but the head noun may optionally get the type-5 suffix `-ʼeʼ` added.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-HQv4n2p5t6-45)

`chuS Saj ngevbogh ghotʼeʼ.`

| chuS        | Saj | ngev | -   | bogh | ghot   | -   | -ʼeʼ |
| ----------- | --- | ---- | --- | ---- | ------ | --- | ---- |
| to be noisy | pet | sell | +   | REL  | person | +   | FOC  |

The person who sells the pet is noisy

`chuS Sajʼeʼ ngevbogh ghot.`

| chuS     | Saj | -   | -ʼeʼ | ngev | -   | bogh | ghot   |
| -------- | --- | --- | ---- | ---- | --- | ---- | ------ |
| be noisy | pet | +   | FOC  | sell | +   | REL  | person |

The pet which the person sells is noisy

Since there is already a type-5 noun suffix marking the head noun, nothing other than the subject or the object can be marked as head noun. Two sentences are formed instead to form the same idea. Relative clauses can have nouns with type 5 suffixes as modifiers, but it can be ambiguous as they can be misinterpreted as being part of the main sentence.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-HQv4n2p5t6-45)

### Purpose clauses

A purpose clause expresses the reason or goal of the action of the main clause. If it is modifying a noun it states the purpose of the noun. A purpose clause always goes before the clause or noun it modifies.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-Okrand_1992,_p.64-44) This is the cause of some grammatical ambiguity in Klingon, as a `-meH` modifying a noun at the beginning of a sentence can be misinterpreted as modifying the entire sentence. This can be resolved in writing with punctuation.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-HQv4n2p5t6-45)

- `-meH` with sentence:

`jumermeH, bISoʼʼegh`

| ju     | -   | mer      | -   | meH,    | bI  | -   | Soʼ  | -   | ʼegh |
| ------ | --- | -------- | --- | ------- | --- | --- | ---- | --- | ---- |
| you-us | +   | surprise | +   | purpose | you | +   | hide | +   | self |

You hid yourself in order to surprise us.

- `-meH` with nouns:

`QaQ vIDubmeH qechlIj`

| QaQ     | vI   | -   | Dub     | -   | meH     | qech | -   | lIj  |
| ------- | ---- | --- | ------- | --- | ------- | ---- | --- | ---- |
| be good | I-it | +   | improve | +   | purpose | idea | +   | your |

Your idea on how I should improve it is good/Your idea for improving it is good. (lit. Your idea [for I improve it] is good)

## Comparatives

In this section, [noun phrases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noun_phrase "Noun phrase") are indicated by the abbreviation _NP_, and adjectives by _A_.

Klingon [comparatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative "Comparative") mainly rely on adjectives like `lawʼ` (_to be many_), `puS` (_to be few_), `rap` (_to be the same_), and `rur` (_to resemble, to be like_) to contrast the nouns. However, many (but not all) of the comparatives have unusual word orders that don't parse as regular Klingon sentences.

- The main Klingon comparative structure is _NP1 A `lawʼ` NP2 A `puS`_.  
    The general meaning of this construction is _"NP1_ is more _A_ than _NP2"_.

`qachvam chuʼ lawʼ juHlIj chu' puS.`

|qachvam|
|---|
|this building|

chuʼ

be new

lawʼ

juHlIj

your home

chu'

puS

This building is newer than your home. (lit. this-building new many – home-your new few)

- The structure _NP1 A `**lawʼ Hoch**` A `**puS**`_ is used to form [superlatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superlative "Superlative"), i.e.  
    "NP1 has the most of quality A (= has more of quality A than anything/one else has)".  
    (`**Hoch**` = _all, everyone, everything_)

`**qIDvetlh tlhaQ law' Hoch tlhaQ puS.**`

| qIDvetlh  | tlhaQ    | law' | Hoch | tlhaQ    | puS. |
| --------- | -------- | ---- | ---- | -------- | ---- |
| that joke | be funny |      |      | be funny |      |

That joke is the funniest. (lit. joke-that funny many – everything funny few)

- The inverse structure, _`Hoch` A `lawʼ` NP1 A `puS`_ is used to indicate  
    "NP1 has the least of quality A".[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-Okrand_1992,_p.64-44)

`Hoch quv lawʼ verengnan quv puS.`

| Hoch | quv          | lawʼ | verengnan   | quv | puS. |
| ---- | ------------ | ---- | ----------- | --- | ---- |
|      | be honorable |      | Ferengi<br> |     |      |

Ferengi are the least honorable. (lit. everyone honorable many – Ferengi honorable few)

- The structure _A NP1; NP2 `rur`_ is used to form [similes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simile "Simile"):  
    "NP1 is A; he/she/it resembles NP2".

`ʼIQ rav rur.`

| ʼIQ    | rav   | rur         |
| ------ | ----- | ----------- |
| be sad | floor | to resemble |

He is as sad as a floor. (lit. he is sad; he is like a floor)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-46)

## Questions

A yes–no question in Klingon can be formed by adding the suffix `-ʼaʼ` to the regular form. The word for yes is `HISlaH` or `HIjaʼ` and the word for no is `ghobeʼ`.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-p68-38) Interrogative pronouns go where the answer would normally go, and don't reorder the sentence. Interrogative adverbs go at the beginning of the sentence.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-47)

## Numbers

Klingon uses a base-10 system to count numbers. To form a multiple of 10, 100, 1 000, 1 000 000, the word for the multiple of ten is suffixed to the digit. For example, `chorghmaH` _eighty_ is a combination of the word `chorgh` _eight_ and the number forming suffix `-maH` _ten_.

Larger powers go before smaller powers: `chorghmaH Soch` is _eight-ten seven_. The number suffix `-DIch` is used to form ordinal numbers, and the number suffix `-logh` indicates how many times an action has been repeated: `loSDIch` _fourth_, `waʼmaH chaʼlogh` _twelve times_.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_grammar#cite_note-48)
